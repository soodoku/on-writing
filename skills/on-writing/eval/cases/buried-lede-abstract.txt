For many years, researchers have been interested in the factors that shape voter turnout. Building on this rich and evolving landscape of scholarship, our study delves into the role of social pressure. We collected data from 180,000 households across three states. We used a randomized design with four mailer conditions. The mailers varied in how much social information they disclosed. Our analysis proceeded in several stages, encompassing balance checks, main estimates, and robustness tests. Ultimately, we found that telling people their neighbors would learn whether they voted raised turnout by 8 percentage points—a striking result that underscores the pivotal role of social pressure, highlighting its enduring importance in the electoral landscape. The future looks bright for this research agenda.
